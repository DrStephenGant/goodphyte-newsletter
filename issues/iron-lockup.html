Your healthy training fuel may be hiding your iron

[ Infographic to be added — alt: The phytate-iron lockup ]

You load up on oats, lentils, and nuts because they're the good stuff. They are — but those same plant foods carry phytate, a storage molecule that clamps onto the non-heme iron in your gut and won't let go. A lot of that iron passes straight through you [1][2].

That matters for runners more than almost anyone. Iron carries oxygen to working muscle and powers the mitochondrial enzymes that turn fuel into pace. When stores fall, your ferritin drops first — often while a standard blood panel still reads "normal" — and your legs feel it before the chart does [3]. Iron shortfall is especially common in female endurance athletes, where it can quietly hold back performance [4].

Here's the lever. Phytase is the enzyme that cleaves phytate, freeing the bound iron — along with zinc, magnesium, and calcium — so your body can actually absorb what you ate [1]. Human reviews of dephytinization and added phytase show greater trace-element bioavailability from plant-based meals [2].

The takeaway

  • Pair your high-phytate training meals with a phytase source.
  • Ask your clinician for a ferritin test, not just hemoglobin.
  • Think bioavailability, not just intake — the iron only counts if you absorb it.
