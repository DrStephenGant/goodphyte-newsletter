Iron deficiency without anemia: the test that reads normal

Your bloodwork comes back "normal," but your easy pace feels like work and your finishing kick has gone missing. Here's the gap: anemia is the *last* stop on the iron-loss road, not the first. Long before your hemoglobin drops, your iron stores — measured by ferritin — can run low. That's iron deficiency without anemia, and it's common in athletes [1].

It matters because iron does more than fill red cells. It powers the mitochondrial enzymes that turn fuel into pace and carry oxygen to working muscle. Research on iron-deficient non-anemic endurance athletes shows impaired iron status even while a standard hemoglobin reading still looks fine [2] — and the burden falls hardest on female runners [3].

So a "normal" CBC can miss it entirely. The answer starts with the right test — and the right inputs.

The takeaway

  • Ask your clinician for a ferritin panel, not just hemoglobin.
  • Low ferritin with normal hemoglobin is still worth acting on.
  • Absorption matters as much as intake — phytate in plant foods binds iron, and phytase helps free it so more of what you eat gets in.
