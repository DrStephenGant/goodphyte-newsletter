Going plant-based without starving your minerals

More runners are going plant-forward, and the protein worry is largely settled: well-planned plant-based diets can supply enough total protein and leucine — the amino acid that switches on muscle repair — to support training adaptations [1], and vegan and vegetarian athletes can perform right alongside omnivores [2].

The quieter issue is minerals. The same whole grains, legumes, nuts, and seeds that anchor a plant-based plate are rich in phytate, which binds iron, zinc, and magnesium and lowers how much you actually absorb [3]. Eat more of these foods and you can take in plenty of minerals on paper while absorbing less of them in practice.

That's where phytase earns its place. The enzyme cleaves phytate and frees the bound minerals — the cofactors your aerobic engine and recovery depend on — so a plant-based plate delivers more of what it promises.

The takeaway

  • Protein and leucine are manageable on a plant-based diet.
  • Mineral *absorption*, not intake, is the subtler challenge.
  • Pair high-phytate meals with a phytase source to unlock iron, zinc, and magnesium.
