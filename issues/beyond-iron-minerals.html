Beyond iron: the minerals your grain bowl locks away

Iron gets the headlines, but it isn't the only mineral your training depends on — and it isn't the only one phytate locks up. The same phytic acid in whole grains, legumes, nuts, and seeds that binds iron also clamps onto zinc, magnesium, and calcium. In plant-forward eating patterns, phytate measurably lowers the bioavailability of iron and zinc [1].

That's a problem for runners, because these minerals do real work. Zinc supports immune function and recovery, magnesium drives the ATP reactions behind every stride, and calcium backs the skeleton you pound mile after mile. Eating them isn't the same as absorbing them.

Phytase changes that math. The enzyme cleaves phytate and releases the bound minerals so your body can take them up — human reviews of dephytinization and added phytase show greater trace-element bioavailability from plant meals [2], consistent with phytase improving mineral solubility and availability [3].

The takeaway

  • Think minerals as a system, not just iron.
  • High-phytate, plant-forward meals can under-deliver zinc and magnesium.
  • Pair them with a phytase source to unlock more of what's already on your plate.
