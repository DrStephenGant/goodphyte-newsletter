Women, cycles, and the iron you can't see

For female runners, iron is a monthly math problem. Every cycle, menstrual blood loss carries iron out of the body — and in female athletes, heavier losses track with lower ferritin, the body's iron-storage gauge [1]. Stack training on top — sweat, foot-strike, the demands of hard blocks — and the deficit compounds. It's a big reason iron deficiency is so common among female endurance athletes [2].

The catch is that you can do everything "right" at the dinner table and still come up short, because not all the iron you eat gets absorbed. The plant sources that fill a runner's plate — oats, lentils, greens, seeds — carry phytate, which binds iron in the gut. Phytase is the enzyme that cleaves phytate and frees that bound iron (and other minerals) for absorption [3].

The takeaway

  • Track iron status across your training year, not just once.
  • Ask for ferritin, and flag heavy periods to your clinician.
  • Make the iron you eat count — pair high-phytate meals with a phytase source.
